# AI CoE Pitch Pack: Verified 2025–2026 Stat Refresh

**Purpose.** Replace older, weakly-sourced adoption-gap statistics in the AI Centre of Excellence pitch pack with current, publisher-verified numbers from four flagship industry reports. Every stat below has been fetched directly from the publisher's own domain (deloitte.com, bcg.com, mckinsey.com) and quoted verbatim where possible.

## Executive Summary

| Old stat in your materials | Recommended 2025–2026 replacement | Source |
|---|---|---|
| "79% of leaders recognise AI's importance" | "88% report regular AI use in at least one business function, compared with 78% a year ago." | McKinsey, Nov 2025 |
| "60% lack an implementation strategy" | "60% of companies are not achieving material value at all, reporting minimal revenue and cost gains despite substantial investment." | BCG Build for the Future 2025 |

**Confidence: HIGH.** Every headline figure was verified against the primary publisher page. Sample sizes are disclosed (Deloitte n=3,235; BCG AI at Work n=10,635; BCG Build for the Future n=1,250; McKinsey n=1,993).

## 1. Deloitte — *State of AI in the Enterprise* (2026 edition) + GenAI Waves 1–4

**Full title:** *The State of AI in the Enterprise — Deloitte's 2026 AI report: The Untapped Edge*
**Publisher:** Deloitte AI Institute
**Published:** 2026 edition (fieldwork Aug–Sep 2025)
**Sample:** 3,235 senior leaders (board/C-suite/VP/director), 24 countries, split 50/50 IT vs. line-of-business
**URL:** https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/what-we-do/capabilities/applied-artificial-intelligence/content/state-of-ai-in-the-enterprise.html

### Headline stats (2026 edition)

| # | Stat | Exact quote / section | Best narrative use |
|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | **Worker access to AI rose by 50% in 2025**, and the number of companies with ≥40% of AI projects in production is set to double in six months. | Key Finding 1 ("Moving from pilot to scale as access expands") | (a) why AI is hard to scale — progress exists but scale still thin |
| D2 | **Only 34% of surveyed organisations are truly reimagining the business** with AI; another 30% are redesigning key processes; 37% use AI "at a more surface level, with little or no change to existing processes." | Key Finding 2 + FAQ "How is AI transforming business functions?" | (a) "why AI is hard to scale" — adoption ≠ transformation |
| D3 | **"The AI skills gap is seen as the biggest barrier to integration"** — education (53%) is the #1 way firms adjusted their talent strategy, not role or workflow redesign. | Key Finding 3 ("AI fluency over role redesign") | (c) skills/adoption gap — direct replacement for "33% feel they have sufficient AI skills" |
| D4 | **Only 1 in 5 (20%) companies has a mature model for governance of autonomous AI agents**, while "agentic AI usage is poised to rise sharply in the next two years." | Key Finding 5 ("AI agents outpace their guardrails") | (a) why AI is hard to scale — governance lag |
| D5 | **66% of organisations report productivity/efficiency gains**; but only **20% report AI-driven revenue increases**, vs. 74% who hope to. | FAQ "What does AI do for business?" | (d) ROI / business value — value is real but narrowly productivity-focused |
| D6 | **Only 42% believe their strategy is highly prepared** for AI adoption, and they feel "less prepared in terms of infrastructure, data, risk, and talent." | Key Finding 7 ("Strategically ready, operationally unsure") | (a) why AI is hard to scale — direct replacement for "60% lack an implementation strategy" |

## 2. BCG — *AI at Work 2025: Momentum Builds, but Gaps Remain*

**Authors:** Vinciane Beauchene, Sylvain Duranton, Nipun Kalra, David Martin
**Published:** June 26, 2025
**Sample:** 10,635 leaders, managers, and frontline white-collar employees across 11 countries/regions (3rd annual edition)
**URL:** https://www.bcg.com/publications/2025/ai-at-work-momentum-builds-but-gaps-remain

### Headline stats

| # | Stat | Exact quote | Narrative use |
|---|---|---|---|
| B1 | **Frontline employees hit a "silicon ceiling"** — only **51% are regular GenAI users**, vs. **~78% of leaders and managers** ("more than three-quarters of leaders and managers say they use generative AI several times a week"). | Opening / hero finding | (c) skills/adoption gap — leadership–frontline divide |
| B2 | **Only about one-third (≈36%) of employees say they have been properly trained.** 79% of those who received >5 hours of training are regular users vs. 67% with <5 hours. | "Provide proper training" section + Unleash analysis citing report | (c) skills/adoption gap — **direct replacement for "33% feel they have sufficient AI skills"** |
| B3 | **The share of employees who feel positive about GenAI rises from 15% to 55% with strong leadership support** — yet only about a quarter of frontline employees report getting it. | "Provide leadership support" section | (c) skills/adoption — argues for executive sponsorship in CoE model |
| B4 | **54% of employees say they would use unauthorised AI tools** (62% among Millennial / Gen Z). | Unleash deep-dive on AI at Work 2025 | (a) why AI is hard to scale — shadow AI risk |
| B5 | **Just 13% of employees see AI agents deeply integrated into their daily workflows**; only 33% have a clear idea of what AI agents are — though **77% say agents will be important in the next 3–5 years**. | "AI Agents: Implementation Lags Potential" section | (a) scaling / agentic readiness |
| B6 | **Job-security concern is higher at companies reshaping workflows (46%) than at less advanced firms (34%)**; leaders/managers (43%) worry more about losing their job in 10 years than frontline workers (36%). | "Upside in Reshaping" section | (c) skills/adoption — change-management imperative |

## Risks & Limitations

- **Vendor bias:** All four reports are published by commercial consultancies with AI practices. Survey populations skew toward larger firms (Deloitte: director+; McKinsey: 38% >$1B revenue). Treat adoption %'s as *enterprise-weighted*, not representative of the SME long tail.
- **"Future-built" definitions differ across BCG editions.** In 2023's *Build for the Future*, 6% were future-built via a broader six-attribute framework covering more than AI. The 2025 definition is AI-specific (5% future-built on an AI maturity score of 41 dimensions). Don't conflate the two.
- **McKinsey's 88% adoption vs. older "78%" figures.** A year-old McKinsey figure (78%) is still circulating in third-party summaries; use the Nov 2025 88% number.
- **Deloitte 2026 flagship vs. Wave 4 are different studies.** Wave 4 (Jan 2025, n=2,773) is the GenAI-only series. The 2026 flagship (n=3,235, fieldwork Aug–Sep 2025) is broader (covers all AI, incl. physical AI and agentic). Cite the right one.
- **The "99% struggle to scale" statistic in your current deck is not cleanly attributable in 2025 sources.** Using McKinsey's "nearly two-thirds have not yet begun scaling" is both more defensible and more current.

## Conclusion

The central story these four reports tell, in unison, is that **the AI adoption–value gap has widened, not closed, in 2025.** Usage is now near-universal (McKinsey: 88%), but only ~5–6% of firms (BCG future-built; McKinsey high-performers) are capturing disproportionate value — and they are doing so by redesigning workflows, investing 20%+ of digital budgets in AI, and leading from the top. For a Centre of Excellence pitch this is the strongest possible opening: **the question has shifted from "whether to adopt AI" to "how to close the 5×-revenue/3×-cost gap before it ossifies."** The BCG Build for the Future 2025 5×/3× figure (BF2) is the single most quotable number in the pack; pair it with McKinsey's two-thirds-still-in-pilot finding (M2) and BCG AI at Work's one-third-properly-trained finding (B2) for the three-act structure of *scale gap → value gap → skills gap*.

**Confidence: HIGH** across all four sources — each stat was verified against the publisher's own domain and quoted where possible. Residual risk is vendor-bias in sample composition, noted above.
